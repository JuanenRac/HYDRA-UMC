================================================================================
              STACK A BUS CONNECTOR - 40-PIN PINOUT (PROPOSED)
        Shared connector definition - identical on the Kinematic Brain's
        "STACK A OUT" port AND on every Robot Controller Board's own
                    "STACK A IN" port (parallel bus drop)
================================================================================
STATUS: PROPOSED - not yet verified against real hardware. This connector
carries the physical bus that ties the Kinematic Brain to up to 8 Robot
Controller Boards: +24V power, GND, +5V auxiliary, and the FDCAN1 "STACK A"
differential pair already documented in docs/architecture.md section 4 and
README.md section 6. This ONE pinout is used identically at both ends of the
bus - see "Physical topology" below for how a single shared pinout still
supports 8 boards wired in parallel rather than daisy-chained point-to-point.

This is the concrete pin-level definition of the connector referenced only
generically ("Heavy-copper shrouded connector, 2.54mm pitch") in README.md
section 11 and hardware/PCB/*/BOM.TXT - see section 4 below for how the two
relate.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS CONNECTOR CARRIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+24V power (10 pins), GND (10 pins), +5V auxiliary (4 pins), plus the FDCAN1
differential pair already documented in docs/architecture.md section 4.
SPI here refers to the already-documented STM32H745<->CM5 SPI1 link
(README.md section 10) - this connector does not carry a second SPI bus.
FDCAN1 remains this connector's only command/data bus, consistent with every
other reference to STACK A in this repo (docs/architecture.md sections 1 and
4, README.md section 6).

One signal beyond straight power/data is necessary, because a 40-pin
parallel bus serving 8 independently-populated slots cannot work without it:

  - BOARD_PRESENT: one open-drain pin, pulled low by an installed Robot
    Controller Board, read by the Kinematic Brain to know which of the 8
    slots are physically populated without needing a full FDCAN handshake
    first (useful at power-up, and for HYDRA-UMC-STUDIO's Flasher/Tester to
    show "not installed" vs. "installed but not responding").

SLOT ADDRESSING IS NOT ON THIS CONNECTOR: each Robot Controller Board
carries its own FDCAN1 slot address (0-7) via 3 LOCAL DIP-switch/jumper pins
on the board itself, set by whoever installs it - see
docs/PINOUT_STM32G474_ROBOT_CONTROLLER.TXT section 1c ("BOARD_ID[2:0]"). A
per-position resistor strap or a ripple-propagated addressing scheme were
both considered and rejected: either adds real complexity that a simple,
locally-set DIP switch avoids, and every Robot Controller Board being the
same interchangeable, mass-produced PCB makes deriving identity from
connector position awkward by design. The 3 pins that scheme would have
needed are folded into the general RESERVED pool below instead.

3. PHYSICAL TOPOLOGY - CONFIRMED: a physical stack, not a backplane/harness
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONFIRMED by the project owner: the 8 Robot Controller Boards mount as a
physical STACK - one on top of another - on one side of the Kinematic
Brain's own PCB, not spread across a separate backplane board or connected
by ribbon cables. A stack keeps every board tapping the identical 40 signals
through its own pair of stacking connectors, with nothing routed through any
board's own logic to reach the next one up.

    Kinematic Brain PCB
    +----------------------------+
    |                            |
    |   [ STACK A OUT connector ]|--+  <- this 40-pin definition, base of the stack
    +----------------------------+  |
                                     v
                          +-------------------+
                          | Robot Ctrl Board 1 |  BOARD_ID DIP=0 (installer-set)
                          | [IN, passthrough]  |--+  <- 2 connectors: one mates
                          +-------------------+  |     downward into the board
                                                  v     below, one extends the
                          +-------------------+        SAME 40 signals upward
                          | Robot Ctrl Board 2 |  BOARD_ID DIP=1 (installer-set)
                          | [IN, passthrough]  |--+
                          +-------------------+  |
                                     :            v
                                     :     ... up to 8 boards, DIP=7
                                     :
                          +-------------------+
                          | Robot Ctrl Board 8 |  BOARD_ID DIP=7 (installer-set)
                          | [IN only - top]    |
                          +-------------------+

BOARD_ID is set locally per board (docs/PINOUT_STM32G474_ROBOT_
CONTROLLER.TXT section 1c), NOT derived from stack position by this
connector - the diagram above shows the installer setting each board's DIP
switch to match its intended physical position, a convention worth
following for sanity but not something the hardware enforces or derives on
its own. Nothing stops (and nothing detects) installing boards out of
numeric order; only the address each board's own DIP switch encodes matters
to the FDCAN1 protocol.

Each Robot Controller Board (except the topmost) carries TWO of this same
40-pin connector: one on its underside mating with the board (or the
Kinematic Brain) below it, one on its topside extending the identical 40
signals to whatever mounts above it - a straight-through passthrough, every
pin wired 1:1 between the two connectors on a given board, exactly like a
Raspberry Pi HAT's own stacking header convention (same idea, this
project's own pin count/pinout, not the Pi's). This is what keeps "each
slave board gets a 40-pin connector" and "physically stacked, electrically
parallel" the SAME design decision: every board taps the identical 40
signals via its own pair of stacking connectors, nothing routes through any
board's own logic to reach the next one up.

CAN bus implication: FDCAN1 (CAN1_H/CAN1_L, pins 25-26) is a genuine
multi-drop stack rather than a backplane trace - 120ohm termination
resistors belong at the two electrical ends: the Kinematic Brain's own
STACK A OUT connector, and the TOPMOST populated Robot Controller Board's
own upward-facing connector (populate/depopulate a termination resistor per
board as stack height changes, or fit a keyed terminator plug on top of
however many boards are actually stacked) - standard CAN termination
practice, applied at the two physical ends of the stack.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CONNECTOR PART / MECHANICAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROPOSED: 2x20, 2.54mm-pitch STACKING header/socket pair (not a plain box
header - see section 3's own stack diagram). This is a more specific version
of README.md section 11's "Heavy-copper shrouded connector, 2.54mm pitch"
entry: same pitch, now with an exact pin count, a per-pin current budget (10
parallel contacts per rail), and a real mechanical form factor behind it.
Candidates: 2x20 female stacking headers (the same family Raspberry Pi HATs
use, e.g. Samtec SSQ/ESQ or Adafruit-style long-leg stacking headers) sized
for however many boards' worth of stack height the mechanical design needs
to clear per board, high-current-rated contacts given the 10-pin-parallel
+24V/GND rails. Exact part number and stack pitch (how much vertical
clearance each board needs) TBD at schematic/mechanical-design time - not
decided here.
