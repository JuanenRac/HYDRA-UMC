STATUS: PROPOSED - fills a gap the user explicitly flagged ("se me habra
olvidado cosas") between the previously-documented FDCAN1 STACK A bus
(docs/architecture.md section 4, README.md section 6) and the user's new,
more specific requirement: "conector de 40 pines para conexion en paralelo
de las 8 tarjetas esclavas ... alimentacion +24V de 10 pines, 10 pines
masa, +5V". Not yet verified against real hardware. This ONE pinout is used
identically at both ends of the bus - see "Physical topology" below for how
that reconciles with "en paralelo" (parallel, not daisy-chained point-to-

This supersedes the vague "Heavy-copper shrouded connector, 2.54mm pitch"
line in README.md section 11 / hardware/PCB/*/BOM.TXT - see the bottom of
this file for the exact reconciliation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS CONNECTOR CARRIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per the user's own explicit split: +24V power (10 pins), GND (10 pins),
+5V - plus what already exists on paper for this bus (docs/architecture.md
section 4): the FDCAN1 differential pair. Reading the user's message
carefully, "conexion spi" appears in the same run-on sentence as the STACK A
connector - this is interpreted here as re-confirming the ALREADY-DOCUMENTED
STM32H745<->CM5 SPI1 link (README.md section 10), not a second bus riding on
this connector. FDCAN1 remains this connector's only command/data bus,
consistent with every prior document this session (docs/architecture.md
sections 1 and 4, README.md section 6). If that reading is wrong, tell me
and this file gets a real SPI_STACKA_* pin group instead of the reserved
spares below.

Two things had to be ADDED beyond the user's literal list, because a 40-pin
parallel bus serving 8 independently-populated slots cannot work without
them:

  - SLOT_ID[2:0]: a 3-bit hardware strap, PROPOSED as three pins on the
    connector, each pulled to a different logic level depending on WHICH of
    the 8 physical backplane drops a Robot Controller Board is plugged into
    (e.g. resistor-coded per drop on a shared backplane PCB, or jumper-
    coded on a ribbon harness). This is what lets each board self-identify
    its own N (0-7) at boot and compute its own FDCAN1 slot base ID
    (CAN_ID_STACKA_BASE + N*0x20, docs/architecture.md section 4) instead
    of needing that ID hand-programmed into firmware per board. Without
    this, the addressing scheme already on paper has no way to actually
    assign N to a physical board.
  - BOARD_PRESENT: one open-drain pin, pulled low by an installed Robot
    Controller Board, read by the Kinematic Brain to know which of the 8
    slots are physically populated without needing a full FDCAN handshake
    first (useful at power-up, and for HYDRA-UMC-STUDIO's Flasher/Tester to
    show "not installed" vs. "installed but not responding").

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PHYSICAL TOPOLOGY - reconciling "40-pin connector" with "en paralelo"
   and with "up to 8 slave boards" on one CAN bus
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A single 40-pin header cannot electrically be "8 boards in parallel" by
itself - it is PROPOSED here as the connector STANDARD (identical pin table)
used at 9 physical locations:

    Kinematic Brain "STACK A OUT" (1x, this connector)
        |
        v
    [ STACK A BACKPLANE / harness - not yet designed, out of scope here ]
        |
        +-- Robot Controller Board slot 1 "STACK A IN" (this connector, SLOT_ID=0)
        +-- Robot Controller Board slot 2 "STACK A IN" (this connector, SLOT_ID=1)
        +-- ... up to slot 8 (SLOT_ID=7)

Two realistic ways to build the actual backplane/harness (a real decision
for whoever lays out hardware/PCB/ - not decided here, both are compatible
with this pin table unchanged):

  (a) A dedicated small backplane PCB with 9 of these connectors wired
      dead-straight pin-for-pin (power/GND/SLOT_ID straps/CAN bus all in
      parallel), CAN_H/CAN_L routed as a proper stub-minimized bus trace
      with 120ohm termination resistors populated only at the two physical
      ends of that trace (the Kinematic Brain end, and whichever slot
      connector sits electrically furthest away) - standard CAN bus
      practice, same principle already used in URTC's own CANBUS.TXT.
  (b) A ribbon/multi-drop harness doing the same electrically, with
      termination resistors in small in-line plugs at the two ends instead
      of on a backplane PCB.

Either way, every Robot Controller Board's own connector is the SAME
40-pin definition in this file, which is what makes "conector de 40 pines"
on the slave boards (per the user's own message) and "conexion en paralelo"
on the master board the same design decision, not two different ones.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. CONNECTOR PART / MECHANICAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROPOSED: 2x20, 2.54mm-pitch, shrouded/keyed box header (IDC or THT) - this
UPDATES, not contradicts, README.md section 11's previous "Heavy-copper
shrouded connector, 2.54mm pitch" line, which was written before the pin
COUNT was known. Same pitch and shrouding as originally specced, now with
an exact pin count and a per-pin current budget behind it (10 parallel
contacts per rail instead of a single unspecified "heavy-copper" contact).
Exact part number TBD at schematic time (candidates: Molex KK 2.54mm /
TE MTA-100 series box headers, both readily available with high-current-
rated contacts and board-to-board or board-to-cable variants).
